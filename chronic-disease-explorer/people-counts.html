Every figure behind the people counts.

The explorer restates a relative risk as people: how many of 1,000 in the comparison group have or develop the disease, and how many in the group with the risk factor would. 179 of the 356 links in the counts carry such a figure. This page lists the baseline of each disease, the inputs of each link and the links that carry no figure, with the reason.

The counts are illustrations for one named population. They describe groups of people who differ in one factor and live equally long. They cannot say who will be affected.

How a count is made

  1. A baseline. How common the disease is in a named general population: a lifetime risk where one was published, otherwise a prevalence.
  2. The comparison group. The population includes people with the risk factor. The comparison group's count is set so that the groups, weighted by their shares of the population, add back to the baseline. For a per-step effect the comparison is a person at the usual level. For highest against lowest, the population is taken as three equal groups.
  3. The group with the factor. The odds of the disease in the comparison group are multiplied by the relative effect. Over a lifetime this allows for death from other causes, which the usual formula for a hazard ratio leaves out.
  4. The range. The same steps at both ends of the studies' interval. It covers the relative effect only.
  5. Rounding. Whole people, and tens from 100 upwards.

The method, its sources and its limits are in the framework, section 14.
