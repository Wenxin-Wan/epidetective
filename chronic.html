What the labels mean

Every link carries one of five grades.

The grade says how sure the evidence is that a risk factor causes a disease. It does not say how large the effect is: the estimate in the tool gives the size.

How sure the evidence is that a risk factor causes a disease

What it saysWhat it does not say

  1. Strong

    What it says
    Different kinds of evidence agree that this is a cause, and a reversal is unlikely.
    What it does not say
    How large the effect is. A Strong link can carry a small effect; the estimate in the panel gives the size.

  2. Moderate

    What it says
    Probably a cause: studies agree and show a gradient or a known mechanism, but one strand of proof is still missing.
    What it does not say
    That the question is settled. The missing strand of proof could confirm the link or overturn it.

  3. Suggestive

    What it says
    A consistent association, but confounding or reverse causation has not been ruled out.
    What it does not say
    That the factor causes the disease. Another factor, or an early stage of the disease itself, may explain the link.

  4. Weak

    What it says
    A signal from few, inconsistent or bias-prone studies. It may not hold up.
    What it does not say
    That the factor has no effect. The studies are too few, too inconsistent or too prone to bias to say much either way.

  5. Conflicting

    What it says
    Good evidence points in opposite directions, often observational studies against trials or genetic studies.
    What it does not say
    Which side is right. The panel shows which kinds of study point which way.

  6. Older names

    What it says
    Convincing → Strong · Probable → Moderate · Limited → Weak · Contested → Conflicting. Suggestive kept its name.
    What it does not say
    That a link kept its grade. Version 2.0 also re-graded some links, and the change log lists each one.

No official term. These five grades belong to the Chronic Disease Evidence Explorer, which defines them in its framework. None of them is an IARC category. Framework, section 3.1 →
