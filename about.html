Who builds this

Built by a cancer epidemiologist who works with this evidence every day.

I am Wenxin Wan, a postdoctoral scientist at the International Agency for Research on Cancer in Lyon, the World Health Organization's cancer agency.

I did my PhD at Utrecht University's Institute for Risk Assessment Sciences, studying how exposures at work and in the environment affect cancer risk. That work helped establish the causal role of benzene in lung cancer, and was covered by The New York Times.

Day to day I work with large international cohort studies, following hundreds of thousands of people over many years to see which exposures are followed by disease. At IARC I lead the analysis for OPICO, which pools ten such cohorts across three continents to ask whether long-term opioid use carries a cancer risk, and I advise the Lung Cancer Cohort Consortium (LC3) on how evidence about exposures at work can feed into lung cancer screening.

I have spent years helping to produce evidence like this, and it has long frustrated me how little of it reaches the people it was meant to protect. A finding that never leaves the literature has done only half its job. Closing that distance is worth every hour it takes, and it is why this site exists.

EpiDetective publishes tools for reading evidence about whole populations. They show patterns across large groups of people. For anything concerning your own health, speak to a doctor. Each tool lists its own sources.
EpiDetective is a personal project by Wenxin Wan, produced in a personal capacity. It carries no endorsement from the International Agency for Research on Cancer or the World Health Organization, and the views expressed here belong to the author alone. The underlying scientific sources are cited so that anyone can check them independently.
