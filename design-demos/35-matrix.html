What causes cancer and other chronic diseases, and how sure the evidence is

Free tools built from the IARC Monographs and published reviews by Wenxin Wan, a cancer epidemiologist.

They describe whole populations, not what caused any one person’s illness.

1 / 4

Every pair at a glance

One square for every pair of an exposure and a cancer type. A filled square is an established link; an outlined square is a suspected one. Here the exposures are grouped by kind and the cancer types by organ system.

2 / 4

Sorted by certainty

Known causes (Group 1) come first, then probable causes (Group 2A) and possible causes (Group 2B). The group says how sure IARC is that something can cause cancer in people. It does not say how large the risk is.

3 / 4

Sorted by reach

Now the exposures linked to the most cancer types come first, and so do the cancer types with the most links. One exposure, many cancers: tobacco smoking. One cancer, many causes: lung cancer.

4 / 4

Same certainty, very different size

Tobacco smoking and processed meat are both in Group 1: the evidence that each can cause cancer is equally sure. The risks are not.

100–120in 1,000 smokers get lung cancer by age 75: about 10 to 12 in 100.
4–5in 1,000 lifelong non-smokers do.
18%Each 50 grams of processed meat eaten every day raises the risk of bowel cancer by 18%.

      How sure, not how big

      IARC sorts each agent into one of four groups by how sure it is that the agent can cause cancer in people. Aspartame is in Group 2B.

      1

      Known cause

      The agent can cause cancer in people. This is the group in which IARC is most certain. It does not say how large the risk is.

      2A

      Probable cause

      The agent probably causes cancer in people.

      2B

      Possible cause

      The agent may cause cancer in people.

      3

      Not classifiable

      The evidence does not meet the rules for any other group. It does not mean safe.
