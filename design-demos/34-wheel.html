What causes cancer and other chronic diseases, and how sure the evidence is

Free tools built from the IARC Monographs and published reviews by Wenxin Wan, a cancer epidemiologist.

They describe whole populations, not what caused any one person’s illness.

Respiratory & intrathoracic

The lungs, the larynx, the nose and sinuses

Tobacco smoking, secondhand smoke, asbestos, radon gas and a long roll of workplace exposures all run to this part of the rim.

Digestive organs

From the food pipe to the bowel and the liver

Here arrive alcoholic drinks, processed meat, hepatitis B and C viruses, aflatoxins and the stomach bacterium Helicobacter pylori.

Lymphoid & haematopoietic

The leukaemias, lymphomas and myeloma

Benzene, X-rays and gamma rays, several chemotherapy drugs and viruses such as Epstein–Barr bundle into the blood cancers.

The one lesson

Same certainty, very different size

Tobacco smoking and processed meat are both in Group 1: the evidence that each can cause cancer is equally sure. The risks are not.

100–120
in 1,000 smokers get lung cancer by age 75: about 10 to 12 in 100
4–5
in 1,000 lifelong non-smokers do
18%
rise in the risk of bowel cancer for each 50 grams of processed meat eaten every day

European data. Quoted from the Cancer Evidence Explorer.
