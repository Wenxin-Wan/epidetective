A guide to the evidenceIntroduction

What causes cancer and other chronic diseases, and how sure the evidence is

Free tools built from the IARC Monographs and published reviews by Wenxin Wan, a cancer epidemiologist.

They describe whole populations, not what caused any one person’s illness.

Plate IIGroup 1

How to identify a Group 1 claimField marks

Same certainty, very different size

Tobacco smoking and processed meat are both in Group 1: the evidence that each can cause cancer is equally sure. The risks are not.

  1. 1
    Tobacco smoking: the full bloom

    About 10 to 12 in 100 smokers get lung cancer by age 75. About 4 to 5 in 1,000 lifelong non-smokers do.

  2. 2
    Processed meat: a small bud

    Each 50 grams eaten every day raises the risk of bowel cancer by 18%.

  3. 3
    One name: Group 1, Known cause

    The agent can cause cancer in people. This is the group in which IARC is most certain. It does not say how large the risk is.

Plate IIIThe four labels

Four labels that look alike

Each says how sure the evidence is that something can cause cancer in people. None says how large the risk is.

Look-alikesField marks

Group 1Known cause

The agent can cause cancer in people. This is the group in which IARC is most certain. It does not say how large the risk is.

Group 2AProbable cause

The agent probably causes cancer in people.

Group 2BPossible cause

The agent may cause cancer in people.

Example: aspartame is in this group.

Group 3Not classifiable

The evidence does not meet the rules for any other group.

Caution: it does not mean safe.
