What causes cancer and other chronic diseases, and how sure the evidence is

Free tools built from the IARC Monographs and published reviews by Wenxin Wan, a cancer epidemiologist.

They describe whole populations, not what caused any one person’s illness.

01

Every line is a link IARC has evaluated

Each dot is an exposure, coloured by how sure IARC is that it can cause cancer. Each ring is a type of cancer, larger when more exposures link to it.

02

Tobacco smoking: one exposure, many cancers

A known cause, in Group 1. Almost every one of its lines is solid: studies in people show that it causes these cancers.

03

Lung cancer: one cancer, many causes

Tobacco smoking meets secondhand smoke, asbestos, radon gas, diesel engine exhaust, outdoor air pollution and many workplace exposures here.

04

Group 1 only

Known cause: the agent can cause cancer in people. This is the group in which IARC is most certain. It does not say how large the risk is.

05

Same certainty, very different size

Tobacco smoking and processed meat are both in Group 1: the evidence that each can cause cancer is equally sure. The risks are not.

100–120
in 1,000 smokers get lung cancer by age 75: about 10 to 12 in 100
4–5
in 1,000 lifelong non-smokers do
18%
rise in the risk of bowel cancer for each 50 grams of processed meat eaten every day
