## 2. One idea (the site's core lesson)
Title: Same certainty, very different size
Body: Tobacco smoking and processed meat are both in Group 1: the evidence that each can cause cancer is equally sure. The risks are not.
Figures (exact):
- About 10 to 12 in 100 smokers get lung cancer by age 75 (i.e. 100 to 120 in 1,000).
- About 4 to 5 in 1,000 lifelong non-smokers do.
- Each 50 grams of processed meat eaten every day raises the risk of bowel cancer by 18%.
Source line: European data. Quoted from the Cancer Evidence Explorer.
Link: Seven ideas for reading a health headline →
Optional supporting facts (only if the design shows the evidence scale):
- Group 1, Known cause: the agent can cause cancer in people. This is the group in which IARC is most certain. It does not say how large the risk is.
- Group 2A, Probable cause: the agent probably causes cancer in people.
- Group 2B, Possible cause: the agent may cause cancer in people. (Aspartame is in this group.)
- Group 3, Not classifiable: the evidence does not meet the rules for any other group. It does not mean safe.
