I

Room I: How sure

Robert HookeMicrographia (1665), Schem. XI (cork)Engraving. Photograph: Wellcome Collection, CC BY 4.0

Four groups, from known cause to not classifiable.

  • 1

    Known cause. The agent can cause cancer in people. This is the group in which IARC is most certain. It does not say how large the risk is.

  • 2A

    Probable cause. The agent probably causes cancer in people.

  • 2B

    Possible cause. The agent may cause cancer in people. Aspartame is in this group.

  • 3

    Not classifiable. The evidence does not meet the rules for any other group. It does not mean safe.

II

Room II: Same certainty, very different size

John SnowMap of cholera deaths around the Broad Street pump, Soho (1854)Public domain

Tobacco smoking and processed meat are both in Group 1: the evidence that each can cause cancer is equally sure. The risks are not.

  • 100–120in 1,000smokers get lung cancer by age 75 (about 10 to 12 in 100).
  • 4–5in 1,000lifelong non-smokers do.
  • 18%higher risk of bowel cancer for each 50 grams of processed meat eaten every day.
