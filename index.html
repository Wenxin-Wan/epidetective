A workshop for epidemiological evidence

The evidence on what causes cancer is public. Reading it shouldn't require a PhD.

EpiDetective builds open instruments that turn epidemiological research into something you can interrogate yourself — for patients, clinicians, journalists, and the people who write policy.

What this is

A workshop, not a journal.

Each instrument here takes a body of evidence that already exists — usually rigorous, usually public, usually unreadable — and rebuilds it as something you can question directly. The work draws on cancer epidemiology, environmental and occupational health, causal inference, biostatistics and machine learning.

The aim throughout is to translate without flattening: to make findings usable without quietly discarding the uncertainty that makes them honest.

Who builds this

Built by a working cancer epidemiologist, not a content team.

I am Wenxin Wan, a postdoctoral scientist at the International Agency for Research on Cancer — the World Health Organization's specialised cancer agency, in Lyon. My work sits where cancer epidemiology, causal inference, biostatistics and machine learning meet.

I completed my PhD at Utrecht University's Institute for Risk Assessment Sciences, investigating occupational and environmental exposures and cancer risk. That work contributed evidence supporting the previously underrecognised causal role of benzene in lung cancer.

I build these instruments because the evidence I work with every day is public and rigorous and almost unreadable unless you already know how to read it. That gap seemed worth closing.
